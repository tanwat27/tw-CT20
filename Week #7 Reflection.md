# Week #7 Reflection

This week, I mostly worked on my second project, which for me was the Clock assignment.
[link to project on P5js](https://editor.p5js.org/tanwat27/sketches/s4y-Nya-i)
I continued working with the rotating torus' idea from earlier in the quarter to make it. On the screen, there are three rotating rings, all nestled within eachother. The outermost one represents seconds, the middle one minutes, and the innermost hours. Every second, the seconds ring spins 180 degrees on the Y axis, and changes color between red and green (green on even seconds, red on odd ones). The minutes ring operates similarly, completing a 180 degree turn around the X axis over the course of a minute, where it's blue during even minutes, and purple during odd ones. The hours ring is colored specifically based on whatever hour it is, so there are 24 individual colors designated to all hours of the day. It spins 180 degrees on the Y axis over the course the course of the hour, changing colors to the next color in the sequence when the hour changes. Because all the rings are constantly turning, and the color of the hours ring is different each hour, the clock looks different at every moment throughout the day.
My biggest challenge was figuring out how I could get all the rings to move constantly. I did some research and saw that date.now uses milliseconds, so I used that and divided it to get seconds, minutes, and hours. So the hours are actually counted in milliseconds and that's what allows it to constantly rotate, and so and and so forth with seconds and minutes.
Then I needed to add colors, and did so using a LOT of "if" statements. Within those "if" statements, I used "x === x" to tell the code make comparisons. With the ones I made for each hour color, I'm sure there is probably a simpler/cleaner way to have assigned each hour a certain color, but I just wrote 24 (hr 0-23) different if statements for "if the hour is this, apply this color". For the minutes and seconds, I just wanted the switch from one to the next to be clear, so I made it so that on all even seconds/minutes, the rings were one color, and a different one on the odds. I did this by using a % modulo operator, where I divided by 2 and if the remainder was equal to zero (aka the number was even) to then fill it using x color, and otherwise use a different color.
But I had to use p5js time and not date.now time. I call the p5js "hour()" "minute()" and "second()" commands (assigned to hr, min, and sec), in addition to the date.now time assigned to "s" "m" and "h". If I tried using the date.now ones, nothing would change the colors, but the p5js time commands use whole numbers, so I can divide them or set them to do certain things at certain times. This is necesarry because date.now is using milliseconds and the numbers get all misaligned if that makes sense.
Lastly, another very important thing I learned was "push" and "pop". I was having trouble with rotations getting assigned to everything and not one singular ring. Then I did some research and found that you need to use "push" and "pop" to isolate different components, and apply rotations within those!
